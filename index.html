Our approach

Three things that make an immigration business plan succeed or fail.

I.

Program compliance first

Every IRCC stream and provincial program has specific formatting requirements, mandatory sections, and documentation standards. A plan that looks professional but misses program requirements fails regardless of the underlying business quality.

II.

Economic argument clarity

Canadian immigration business plans must make a compelling case for economic contribution: jobs created, capital invested, and market demand. The financial model and narrative must tell a coherent, quantified story from the first page.

III.

Officer-ready language

Immigration officers read hundreds of plans. Plans written in translated or template language stand out for the wrong reason. Every plan is written in plain, direct Canadian English with the officer's evaluation criteria explicitly addressed throughout.

Immigration streams

Start-Up Visa (SUV)

Federal Start-Up Visa Program

Business plans for founders seeking permanent residence through the SUV program, written to the Letter of Support requirements of designated organizations including incubators, angel groups, and VCs. Covers the full five-year financial model, team narrative, and Canadian market validation.

Provincial Nominee Programs

PNP Entrepreneur Streams

Plans for BCPNP, AINP, OINP, MPNP, and other provincial entrepreneur and investor streams. Each plan is formatted to the specific province's scoring rubric and mandatory documentation checklist, not a generic template adapted after the fact.

Intra-Company Transfer (ICT)

ICT Business Plans

Plans for executives and specialized knowledge workers establishing or transferring to a Canadian entity. Covers the Canadian operations narrative, organizational chart, and economic justification documentation required by IRCC under the ICT work permit category.

Self-Employed & C-11

Self-Employed Business Plans

Plans for artists, athletes, and farm operators applying under the Self-Employed Persons Program, and for entrepreneurs applying under C-11 (owner-operator LMIA exemption). Both streams require a distinctive narrative structure that differs significantly from standard business plans.
